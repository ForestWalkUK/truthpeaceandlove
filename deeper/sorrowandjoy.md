<p><em>All Scripture quotations are from the Authorised (King James) Version. Words in italics are those supplied by the translators.</em></p>
<hr>
<details class="part" id="chapter-i-of-gods-fatherly-providence-in-affliction">
<summary><h2>Chapter I. Of God’s Fatherly Providence in Affliction</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-i-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> Afflictions Appointed by God’s Providence</h3></summary>
<p>God, who orders all things by His most wise and holy providence, appoints the afflictions of His people, whether of body or of mind, in their kind, measure and duration; so that no sorrow befalls a believer by chance, nor apart from the will of his heavenly Father. (Lamentations 3:32-33; Romans 8:28; 1 Peter 1:6)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-i-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> God Chastens as a Father</h3></summary>
<p>God does not afflict willingly, nor delight in the grief of His children, but chastens them as a Father, “for <em>our</em> profit, that <em>we</em> might be partakers of his holiness”. (Lamentations 3:33; Hebrews 12:6, 10-11)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-i-3">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">3.</span> Afflictions Are Marks of Sonship</h3></summary>
<p>Afflictions are therefore not tokens of God’s rejection but marks of sonship, laid upon believers only “for a season, if need be”. (Hebrews 12:6; 1 Peter 1:6)</p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="chapter-ii-of-the-nature-of-sorrow-and-downcastedness">
<summary><h2>Chapter II. Of the Nature of Sorrow and Downcastedness</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-ii-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> Sorrow Not in Itself Sin</h3></summary>
<p>Sorrow is not in itself sin. Man being made of body and soul, heaviness may arise from bodily weakness, from outward trouble, from temptation, or from the hiding of God’s countenance, as well as from sin; and these causes are often mingled together. (Psalm 42:5, 7; 2 Corinthians 1:8; Isaiah 50:10; Nehemiah 2:2-3)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-ii-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> Sorrow Mixed with Remaining Corruption</h3></summary>
<p>Yet through the corruption remaining in us, sorrow may become mixed with unbelief, murmuring, self-pity and slavish fear; and these are to be confessed and mortified, while the affliction itself is borne in humble submission. (Philippians 2:14; Psalm 77:7-10)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-ii-3">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">3.</span> Reasoning with One’s Own Soul</h3></summary>
<p>The godly are neither to deny their downcastedness nor to be governed by it, but, after the example of the Psalmist, to reason with their own souls and to charge them to hope in God: “Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and <em>why</em> art thou disquieted in me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him <em>for</em> the help of his countenance.” (Psalm 42:5, 11)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-ii-4">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">4.</span> The Use of Ordinary Means</h3></summary>
<p>Melancholy and depression of spirit, being often bodily in part, may lawfully be relieved by the use of ordinary means, such as sleep, food and drink, rest and suitable care, together with the means of grace; as the Lord dealt with Elijah under the juniper tree, giving him sleep and sending His angel to say, “Arise <em>and</em> eat; because the journey <em>is</em> too great for thee”. The use of such means is no want of faith, for the Lord “knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we <em>are</em> dust”. (1 Kings 19:4-8; 1 Timothy 5:23; Psalm 103:14)</p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="chapter-iii-of-christ-the-man-of-sorrows">
<summary><h2>Chapter III. Of Christ, the Man of Sorrows</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-iii-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> Christ’s True Sorrows</h3></summary>
<p>The Lord Jesus Christ, in the days of His humiliation, was “a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief”, truly sorrowing in His human soul, both under the sins of His people laid upon Him and over the miseries of men. (Isaiah 53:3; Matthew 26:37-38; John 11:35; Luke 19:41)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-iii-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> Christ’s Sinless Sorrow and Joy</h3></summary>
<p>Yet His sorrows were wholly without sin, never exceeding due measure nor rising against the will of His Father; and with them He knew joy, rejoicing in spirit at His Father’s will, and enduring the cross “for the joy that was set before him”. (Hebrews 4:15; Luke 10:21; Hebrews 12:2)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-iii-3">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">3.</span> Our Sympathising High Priest</h3></summary>
<p>He is therefore a merciful and sympathising High Priest, who can “be touched with the feeling of our infirmities”, who will not break the bruised reed nor quench the smoking flax, and who calls the heavy laden to Himself for rest. (Hebrews 4:15; Isaiah 42:3; Matthew 11:28)</p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="chapter-iv-of-the-love-of-god-towards-believers">
<summary><h2>Chapter IV. Of the Love of God towards Believers</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-iv-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> God’s Free and Everlasting Love</h3></summary>
<p>God loves His elect with an everlasting love, freely in Christ, and not for anything foreseen in them; whereby He has made them “accepted in the beloved”. (Jeremiah 31:3; Ephesians 1:6; 1 John 4:19)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-iv-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> The Unchangeableness of God’s Love</h3></summary>
<p>This love is unchangeable, and nothing present or to come “shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord”. (Romans 8:38-39)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-iv-3">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">3.</span> The Fruits of Knowing God’s Love</h3></summary>
<p>The knowledge and sense of this love, shed abroad in the heart by the Holy Ghost, casts out slavish fear, delivers from the snare of the fear of man, and is the spring of all true love to God and to men: “We love him, because he first loved us.” (Romans 5:5; Romans 8:15; 1 John 4:18-19; Proverbs 29:25)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-iv-4">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">4.</span> Growing in the Knowledge of His Love</h3></summary>
<p>Believers are therefore to labour, by meditation on the Word, prayer, the sacraments and the communion of saints, that being “rooted and grounded in love”, they may increasingly know “the love of Christ, which passeth knowledge”. (Ephesians 3:17-19; Zephaniah 3:17)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-iv-5">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">5.</span> The Warmth of a Soul Established in Love</h3></summary>
<p>A soul thus established in the love of God is made gentle, generous and patient towards others, being freed from the anxious seeking of its own esteem. (1 John 4:18-19; John 3:30)</p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="chapter-v-of-assurance-in-darkness">
<summary><h2>Chapter V. Of Assurance in Darkness</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-v-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> Assurance Shaken but Not Lost</h3></summary>
<p>True believers may have the sense of God’s love shaken, diminished and intermitted, through bodily distempers, manifold temptations, falling into sin, or God’s withdrawing the light of His countenance; yet they are never utterly destitute of the seed of God and the life of faith, by which, in due time, assurance may be revived. (Isaiah 50:10; Psalm 77:7-10; 1 John 3:9)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-v-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> Resting on the Promise in Darkness</h3></summary>
<p>In such seasons the believer is to rest upon the promise rather than upon his feelings, and, though he walk “<em>in</em> darkness, and hath no light”, to “trust in the name of the LORD, and stay upon his God”, waiting for the morning. (Isaiah 50:10; Psalm 30:5; Lamentations 3:22-23)</p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="chapter-vi-of-spiritual-joy">
<summary><h2>Chapter VI. Of Spiritual Joy</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-vi-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> The Nature of Spiritual Joy</h3></summary>
<p>Spiritual joy is a fruit of the Spirit, distinct from natural cheerfulness of temper; it is grounded not in outward circumstances nor in present feelings, but in God Himself and His covenant in Christ. (Galatians 5:22; Philippians 4:4; Nehemiah 8:10; Habakkuk 3:17-18)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-vi-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> Joy and Heaviness Together</h3></summary>
<p>This joy may and does coexist with heaviness, so that the believer may be “As sorrowful, yet alway rejoicing”. (2 Corinthians 6:10; 1 Peter 1:6)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-vi-3">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">3.</span> A Deep and Grounded Joy</h3></summary>
<p>Wherefore a believer, though cast down in feeling, may truly and without dissembling profess a deep and grounded joy in Christ: deep, because it lies beneath the changing surface of his feelings; and grounded, because it rests not upon himself but upon Christ and the love of God in Him, so beautifully revealed in the Holy Scriptures. In so speaking he declares not the passing state of his feelings, but his abiding portion. (Habakkuk 3:18; Ephesians 3:17; Romans 8:38-39; Romans 15:4)</p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="chapter-vii-of-speech-and-demeanour-in-sorrow">
<summary><h2>Chapter VII. Of Speech and Demeanour in Sorrow</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-vii-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> The Duty of Truthfulness</h3></summary>
<p>The ninth commandment requires that believers speak the truth from the heart; they may not therefore profess a gladness of feeling which they do not have. (Ephesians 4:25; Psalm 15:2)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-vii-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> Seasonable and Edifying Speech</h3></summary>
<p>Yet they are not bound to declare all their griefs to all men, but are to speak seasonably, choosing what is “good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers”; for “Heaviness in the heart of man maketh it stoop: but a good word maketh it glad.” (Ephesians 4:29; Proverbs 12:25; Colossians 4:6)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-vii-3">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">3.</span> Sorrow Not to Be Paraded</h3></summary>
<p>To parade sorrow before men, that one may be seen and pitied, is condemned by Christ; the believer is rather to wash his face and keep his heaviness before his Father in secret, who sees in secret. Such reserve is not hypocrisy but obedience. (Matthew 6:16-18)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-vii-4">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">4.</span> Against Ostentation</h3></summary>
<p>Neither ostentatious mirth nor ostentatious gloom becomes a Christian, for both set self where Christ should be seen: “He must increase, but I <em>must</em> decrease.” (John 3:30)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-vii-5">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">5.</span> Answering Truly When Asked</h3></summary>
<p>When sincerely asked, the believer may answer truly concerning his sorrow, as Nehemiah did before the king; for reserve is not deceit, nor is honest answer a want of self-control. (Nehemiah 2:2-3)</p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="chapter-viii-of-the-communion-of-saints-in-sorrow">
<summary><h2>Chapter VIII. Of the Communion of Saints in Sorrow</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-viii-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> Opening the Heart to God and the Brethren</h3></summary>
<p>Believers may and ought to pour out their hearts fully before God, and to open their griefs to fit and faithful brethren, as Christ took three of His disciples with Him in Gethsemane. (Psalm 62:8; Matthew 26:37-38; James 5:16)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-viii-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> Bearing One Another’s Burdens</h3></summary>
<p>Christ has appointed His people to “Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ”; to comfort the feebleminded, support the weak, and be patient toward all; to weep with them that weep, and not to sing songs to a heavy heart. (Galatians 6:2; 1 Thessalonians 5:14; Romans 12:15; Proverbs 25:20)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-viii-3">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">3.</span> The Church a Family for the Solitary</h3></summary>
<p>“God setteth the solitary in families”; and the church, as the household of faith, is the ordinary means by which those deprived of earthly love may have the love of God made visible to them. (Psalm 68:6; Mark 10:29-30; Galatians 6:10)</p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="chapter-ix-of-natural-temperament-and-the-family">
<summary><h2>Chapter IX. Of Natural Temperament and the Family</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-ix-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> Diversity of Temperament</h3></summary>
<p>God forms each person in body and temper as it pleases Him, giving diverse dispositions, some more sociable, some more retiring, some more sensitive, some more steady; all of which are His workmanship, and serve together in the one body. (Psalm 139:13-14; 1 Corinthians 12:14-21)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-ix-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> Grace Sanctifies Temperament</h3></summary>
<p>Grace does not abolish nature but renews and sanctifies it; yet no temperament excuses sin, nor exempts anyone from any duty or any part of the fruit of the Spirit. (2 Corinthians 5:17; Galatians 5:22-23; James 1:19)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-ix-3">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">3.</span> Upbringing and Grace</h3></summary>
<p>The godly upbringing of children in a loving household is a great blessing and means, and parents are earnestly to attend to it; yet it is not the ground of salvation, nor is the want of it any bar to grace, for “When my father and my mother forsake me, then the LORD will take me up.” (Ephesians 6:4; Deuteronomy 6:7; 2 Timothy 1:5; Psalm 27:10; John 1:13)</p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="chapter-x-of-witness-before-them-that-are-without">
<summary><h2>Chapter X. Of Witness before Them That Are Without</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-x-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> Adorning the Doctrine</h3></summary>
<p>Believers labouring among unbelievers are to “adorn the doctrine of God our Saviour in all things”, by faithful work, gracious speech, the avoiding of murmuring, and readiness to give a reason of their hope “with meekness and fear”. (Titus 2:10; Colossians 4:5-6; Philippians 2:14-15; 1 Peter 3:15)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-x-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> Testimony in Affliction</h3></summary>
<p>In their afflictions they are neither to burden others needlessly nor to feign, but by a steady, gentle and truthful demeanour to let their light so shine that men may glorify their Father which is in heaven. (Matthew 5:16; Philippians 2:15)</p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="chapter-xi-of-the-mercy-and-fruit-of-affliction">
<summary><h2>Chapter XI. Of the Mercy and Fruit of Affliction</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-xi-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> Afflictions Sanctified Are Mercies</h3></summary>
<p>Afflictions, being sanctified, are mercies: weaning from the world, humbling pride, driving to prayer and to Christ, teaching His statutes, fitting believers to comfort others, and working for them “a far more exceeding <em>and</em> eternal weight of glory”. (Psalm 119:71, 75; 2 Corinthians 12:9; 2 Corinthians 1:4; 2 Corinthians 4:17; Hebrews 12:11)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-xi-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> A Testimony to Men</h3></summary>
<p>The afflictions of believers are often appointed not for their own sakes only, but for the sake of others, that “the works of God should be made manifest” in them. As God set forth His servant Job, and as Paul’s bonds fell out “rather unto the furtherance of the gospel”, so the patience, faith and hope of believers under trial may be beheld by unbelievers and by the brethren alike, to the glory of God, the strengthening of the saints, and the drawing of sinners to Christ; for the treasure is in earthen vessels, “that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us”. Yet to those who behold it but persist in unbelief to the end of their lives, the same testimony will ultimately prove, on that Final Day, to have been “an evident token of perdition”, a “savour of death unto death”, and “a testimony against them”, leaving them with no cloak for their sin and witnessing to the justice of their final condemnation, as Noah by his faith “condemned the world”. Since believers cannot know who are the reprobate, they are not to judge in this, but to bear their testimony to all in love, leaving the issue to God. (John 9:3; Job 1:8; Philippians 1:12-14; Acts 16:25; 2 Corinthians 4:7-11; 1 Peter 2:12; Genesis 50:20; Philippians 1:28; 2 Corinthians 2:15-16; Matthew 10:18; John 15:22; Hebrews 11:7; 2 Thessalonians 1:4-6)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-xi-3">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">3.</span> Falls Overruled for the Good of Others</h3></summary>
<p>God may even overrule the falls of His children, and their subsequent repentance, for the good of others, so that, being restored, they may strengthen their brethren and teach transgressors His ways, and that in them Christ may “shew forth all longsuffering, for a pattern to them which should hereafter believe on him”. Yet their falls are in themselves sinful, and give “great occasion to the enemies of the LORD to blaspheme”; and none may presume upon this mercy, nor continue in sin that grace may abound. (Luke 22:31-32; Psalm 51:13; 1 Timothy 1:16; Psalm 40:3; 2 Samuel 12:14; Romans 3:8; Romans 6:1-2)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-xi-4">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">4.</span> A Testimony to Angels and Devils</h3></summary>
<p>The trials of believers are a testimony not to men only, but also to angels; for believers are “made a spectacle unto the world, and to angels, and to men”. The elect angels, beholding the faith, patience and perseverance of the saints, and their repentance when fallen, learn thereby “the manifold wisdom of God”, and are moved to rejoice and to give Him praise. The devils also are witnesses of these things; and though Satan may for a time prevail against believers, as when he sifted Peter, yet as Job’s steadfastness confounded the accusations of Satan, so the perseverance of believers ultimately puts to shame the accuser of the brethren, displays his overthrow by the blood of the Lamb, and manifests the justice of that everlasting fire “prepared for the devil and his angels”. (1 Corinthians 4:9; Ephesians 3:10; 1 Peter 1:12; Luke 15:10; 1 Timothy 5:21; Luke 22:31-32; Job 1:8-11; Job 2:3, 10; Revelation 12:10-11; Colossians 2:15; Jude 6; Matthew 25:41)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-xi-5">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">5.</span> Prayer for Deliverance and the Use of Means</h3></summary>
<p>The acknowledgement of the mercy that is in affliction does not forbid the believer to pray for deliverance, as Paul besought the Lord thrice, nor to use lawful means of relief; for submission to the Father’s hand and the use of His appointed means are both acts of faith. (2 Corinthians 12:8-9; 1 Timothy 5:23)</p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="chapter-xii-of-the-end-of-all-sorrow">
<summary><h2>Chapter XII. Of the End of All Sorrow</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="chapter-xii-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">1.</span> Sorrow but for a Moment</h3></summary>
<p>The sorrows of believers are but for a moment, and “weeping may endure for a night, but joy <em>cometh</em> in the morning”. (Psalm 30:5; 2 Corinthians 4:17)</p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="chapter-xii-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no">2.</span> Everlasting Joy</h3></summary>
<p>At the last, the ransomed of the LORD shall obtain everlasting joy, “and sorrow and sighing shall flee away”; for “God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.” (Isaiah 35:10; Revelation 21:4)</p>
</details>
</details>

<p><em>Chapter V, section 1, borrows its wording in part from the Westminster Confession of Faith, chapter 18, section 4, and Chapter VII, section 1, draws on the Westminster Larger Catechism’s teaching on the ninth commandment. Those standards were set forth by the Westminster Assembly and received by Presbyterian churches; this Confession is a private statement, and carries none of the authority of the church. It seeks only to bear witness to the truth, which is established by the Holy Scriptures it cites.</em></p>

<h2 id="how-this-confession-was-written">How this Confession was written</h2>
<p>This Confession was written by Carl Hamblin with the help of Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic. It grew out of a single conversation in which I put eight rounds of questions to Claude from a Puritan, Reformed and Covenanter perspective: whether giving cheerful words while sad is hypocrisy; the Lord Jesus Christ as the “man of sorrows”; whether “grounded joy” might be a better phrase than “deep joy”; maintaining a good testimony among unbelievers; the warmth that comes from knowing one is deeply loved; the importance of a stable, loving upbringing; personality and temperament; and the necessary mercy in afflictions. Several of its central thoughts came from my own contributions, including a Christian brother’s answer of “deep joy” when asked how he was.</p>
<p>From that material Claude drew up a first draft, which I then revised over five further rounds: removing a reference in Chapter II, section 4, and adding “deep joy” alongside “grounded joy”; adding sleep, food and drink, after the Lord’s dealing with Elijah; adding that the believer’s joy rests upon the love of God “so beautifully revealed in the Holy Scriptures”; adding, at my suggestion, two new sections to Chapter XI on afflictions as a testimony to others, including the falls and repentance of believers, and the testimony borne against those who persist in unbelief to the end; and adding a further section to Chapter XI, also at my suggestion, on the trials of believers as a testimony to the elect angels and to the devils. Claude helped with historical and theological background from the Puritan and Confessional writers, with drafting and refining the wording, and with checking every Scripture quotation, including the translators’ italics, against the Authorised (King James) Version. All of the content has been checked and approved by me, and the final editorial decisions are mine.</p>
<p>For all that is in accordance with His truth, the glory belongs to God alone, through our Lord Jesus Christ. “Not unto us, O LORD, not unto us, but unto thy name give glory, for thy mercy, <em>and</em> for thy truth’s sake” (Psalm 115:1).</p>
