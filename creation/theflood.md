Most people know the outline of this story from childhood: a great boat, the animals going in two by two, a rainbow at the end. It is often told as a tale for children. The Bible tells it as history, and as both a warning and a mercy that concern every one of us.

## whathappened

The account is in Genesis, the first book of the Bible, in chapters 6 to 9. It says that the earth had become filled with violence, and that the wickedness of man was great. God is holy, which means He is wholly pure and set against all evil, and He determined to bring a flood of waters upon the earth.

Yet before the judgment came, there was grace. Grace is God’s kindness to people who have not earned it.

> But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD. (Genesis 6:8, KJV)

God told Noah to build an ark, a great wooden vessel, and gave him its measurements. Noah built it as he was told, and the Bible calls him “a preacher of righteousness” (2 Peter 2:5): while the ark was being built, he was warning the people around him. God waited patiently all that time.

## thedoor

The ark had one door, set in its side (Genesis 6:16). When the time came, God called Noah in:

> And the LORD said unto Noah, Come thou and all thy house into the ark; for thee have I seen righteous before me in this generation. (Genesis 7:1, KJV)

Noah went in with his family and the animals. Then the Bible records one short sentence: “and the LORD shut him in” (Genesis 7:16, KJV).

Notice who closed the door. It was not Noah. Those inside were kept by God’s own hand, and those outside could not open it. Everything depended on which side of that one door a person was standing when the rain began.

## thedaysofnoah

The Lord Jesus Christ spoke of the Flood as a real event, and He used it to speak about the future:

> For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, And knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be. (Matthew 24:38-39, KJV)

In these words the Lord Jesus did not describe their violence. He described ordinary life: meals, weddings, the daily round. His point was that the judgment found them unprepared. They had heard the warning, and they did not come in.

The Bible says that the same holy God has appointed a day on which He will judge the world, by the Lord Jesus Christ, whom He raised from the dead (Acts 17:31). Each of us will stand there, and the Bible’s verdict on us all is plain:

> For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God; (Romans 3:23, KJV)

That includes the man who wrote this page.

## thedoornow

The ark was a rescue, and it was also a picture of a greater rescue. The Lord Jesus said:

> I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture. (John 10:9, KJV)

Jesus Christ is the Son of God. He came into the world, lived without sin, and died on the cross, bearing the punishment that sinners deserve. On the third day He rose again from the dead. Everyone who comes to Him is safe in Him, as Noah was safe in the ark.

That door is still open, and God’s patience is the reason:

> The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance. (2 Peter 3:9, KJV)

Repentance means turning: a change of mind about God and about your sin, which turns you from your sin to Him. Noah was not saved because he was better than his neighbours; the Bible says he “found grace”, and that he believed God’s warning (Hebrews 11:7). Nobody is told to make himself good enough first. The Lord Jesus said, “him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out” (John 6:37, KJV).

So the question this story puts to you is not how much you know about boats or rainfall. It is which side of the door you are on. Come to Christ as you are, and ask Him to receive you. He has said that He will.
