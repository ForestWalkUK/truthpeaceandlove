> **“Therefore I say unto you, What things soever ye desire, when ye pray, believe that ye receive *them*, and ye shall have *them*.”**
>
> — Mark 11:24

Few verses in the Bible are quoted as often, or in as many different ways, as this one. Law of Attraction and manifesting teachers often cite it as though Jesus were teaching that focused belief, on its own, has the power to shape outcomes: if you can feel that something is already yours, it must come to you. The Word of Faith movement often reads it in a similar way, as though a believer’s confident faith and spoken words could secure health, prosperity or whatever is asked.

Taken on its own, out of context, I can see why the verse might be read like that. But I don’t believe that is what Jesus meant, and I believe what He actually meant is far better news.

This catechism is my attempt to explain the verse biblically, letting the events around it in Mark chapter 11, and the rest of Scripture, show what Jesus was saying. It follows the question-and-answer pattern of the Westminster catechisms, but in plain modern English, with Bible references after each answer so that you can check everything for yourself. Whether you are a Christian, someone exploring manifesting, or simply curious, you are very welcome here. If you are reading on a desktop computer, you can hover over any Bible reference to read the verse itself.

<details class="part" id="the-setting-jerusalem-and-gods-patience">
<summary><h2>The setting: Jerusalem, and God’s patience</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="q-1">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 1.</span> What does Jesus promise in Mark 11:24?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Jesus promises His followers that whatever they ask for in prayer, trusting that they receive it, they will have.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 11:24.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-2">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 2.</span> When and where did Jesus make this promise?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Jesus made this promise in Jerusalem, in the last week before His death. He had just entered the city as its promised King, cursed a fig tree that was full of leaves but bore no fruit, and driven the traders out of the temple.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Zechariah 9:9; Mark 11:1-21.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-3">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 3.</span> Who was it that came to Jerusalem that week?</h3></summary>
<p>A. It was the Lord Jesus Christ, the eternal Son of God, who became man. He came not to judge the world but to save it: to be crucified for sinners, and to rise again from the dead the third day. Yet He came also as the Lord of His temple, and He wept over the city as He warned it of the judgement that would fall on it for rejecting Him.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Malachi 3:1; John 1:14; Galatians 4:4; 1 Timothy 3:16; John 3:17; John 12:47-48; Mark 10:33-34; Luke 19:41-44.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-4">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 4.</span> Why had God formed the nation of Israel?</h3></summary>
<p>A. God called Abraham and promised that through his family all the families of the earth would be blessed. Israel was the nation through which God would give His Word to the world, and from which the Saviour Himself would be born.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Genesis 12:1-3; Romans 3:1-2; Romans 9:4-5.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-5">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 5.</span> How did God show His patience towards Israel?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Over many centuries, God rescued Israel from slavery in Egypt, gave them a good land, and kept sending His prophets to call them back to Himself, early and late, because He had compassion on them. For many years He bore with them.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Exodus 20:2; Jeremiah 7:25; 2 Chronicles 36:15; Nehemiah 9:30; Isaiah 5:1-4.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-6">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 6.</span> How did Israel respond to God’s patience?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Again and again they turned away. Within weeks of leaving Egypt they worshipped a golden calf. Later they served other gods, even sacrificing their own children to them. They mocked God’s messengers, despised His words, and killed many of His prophets.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Exodus 32:1-8; Jeremiah 7:31; Psalm 106:37-38; 2 Chronicles 36:16; Nehemiah 9:26; Acts 7:51-52.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-7">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 7.</span> How did they respond when God’s own Son came to them?</h3></summary>
<p>A. They rejected Him too. He came to His own, and His own did not receive Him. His home town tried to throw Him from a cliff; the religious leaders said His miracles were the work of the devil, and for a long time they had been plotting to kill Him. On the very day He cleansed the temple, they looked for a way to destroy Him.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: John 1:11; Luke 4:28-30; Mark 3:6, 22; John 11:53; Mark 11:18.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-8">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 8.</span> What did they do in the days that followed?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Within days they had Him arrested, condemned and crucified, just as He had foretold in His parable of the vineyard owner’s beloved son. Yet even on the cross He prayed for them, “Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do,” and soon afterwards thousands of them repented and were saved.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 12:1-9; Mark 14:1, 43-64; Mark 15:12-15; Luke 23:34; Acts 2:23, 36-41.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-9">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 9.</span> What does the withered fig tree picture?</h3></summary>
<p>A. It pictures God’s judgement on a people who looked religious outwardly but bore no fruit for God, after all His patience. That judgement came in two stages: God gave the nation over to the blindness it had chosen, and later, in A.D. 70, Jerusalem and its temple were destroyed. Yet God did not cast away every one of them.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Jeremiah 8:13; John 12:37-40; Luke 19:43-44; Romans 11:1-2.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-10">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 10.</span> What does this history mean for us?</h3></summary>
<p>A. It is a mirror for every one of us. God shows the same patience to us, and His kindness is meant to lead us to turn back to Him. But His patience is not endless, and it is not to be presumed upon. While He waits, His call stands open: “To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts.”</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Romans 2:4-5; Romans 11:20-22; Hebrews 3:15; 2 Peter 3:15.</em></p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="faith-in-god">
<summary><h2>Faith in God</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="q-11">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 11.</span> What does the withered fig tree teach us about this promise?</h3></summary>
<p>A. The fig tree withered at Jesus’ word alone. It shows that the power behind this promise is God’s power, not ours, and that our trust is to be in God Himself, not in how strongly we believe.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 11:20-23; Psalm 33:9; Luke 1:37.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-12">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 12.</span> What is the first thing Jesus asks of those who pray?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Jesus begins with the words “Have faith in God.” Faith in God, rather than in ourselves or in the universe, is where all true prayer starts, for without faith it is impossible to please Him.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 11:22; Hebrews 11:6; Romans 1:25.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-13">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 13.</span> Is this promise made to everyone?</h3></summary>
<p>A. No. Jesus made this promise to His own disciples, who trusted in Him. The Bible is honest that God does not welcome the prayers of those who turn their backs on Him and His Word. That is a sobering truth, and by nature it is true of every one of us.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 11:22; John 14:13-14; Proverbs 15:8; Proverbs 28:9.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-14">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 14.</span> Is there any prayer God will hear from someone who does not yet know Him?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Yes, and gladly. God has promised to hear anyone, from anywhere, who turns to Him and asks for mercy, like the tax collector who prayed, “God be merciful to me a sinner.” No one who comes to Him in that way is ever turned away.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Luke 18:13-14; Romans 10:13; Isaiah 55:6-7; John 6:37.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-15">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 15.</span> Why does God accept the prayers of those who trust Him?</h3></summary>
<p>A. God accepts them only because of what Jesus has done. Jesus took upon Himself the curse that our wrongdoing deserved, so that all who trust Him can come to God as their Father.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Galatians 3:13; John 14:13-14; Ephesians 2:18; Hebrews 4:14-16.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-16">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 16.</span> What should we ask for in prayer?</h3></summary>
<p>A. The whole Bible guides us in prayer, but the special rule is the prayer Jesus taught His disciples, called the Lord’s Prayer. It may be prayed as it stands, with understanding and faith, and it is also the pattern for all our other prayers. It teaches us to ask first for the things that matter most to God: that His name be hallowed, that is, honoured as holy; that His kingdom come; and that His will be done on earth, as it is in heaven. Then it teaches us to ask for what we need: our daily bread, the forgiveness of our sins as we forgive others, and to be kept from temptation and delivered from evil. Following that pattern, we may ask for anything that serves God’s glory and the good of His people, for others as well as for ourselves, but never for anything His Word forbids.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Matthew 6:9-13, 33; Luke 11:1-4; 1 Timothy 2:1-2; Philippians 2:4; 1 John 5:14.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-17">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 17.</span> Where do right desires in prayer come from?</h3></summary>
<p>A. They come from God Himself. He works in His people, shaping both what they want and what they do, and His Spirit helps them to pray when they don’t know what to ask.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Philippians 2:13; Psalm 10:17; Romans 8:26-27.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-18">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 18.</span> Are all the desires of Christians from God?</h3></summary>
<p>A. No. Christians still struggle with wrong desires, and their prayers are often a mixture of what God has put in their hearts and what they simply want for themselves. Even Peter, who loved Jesus dearly, was once rebuked for seeing things from a merely human point of view.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 8:33; Galatians 5:17; James 4:3.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-19">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 19.</span> What does it mean to “believe that ye receive”?</h3></summary>
<p>A. It means trusting God Himself, His power, goodness, wisdom and faithfulness, rather than the strength of our feelings or the outcome we hope for. It is being confident that He hears us, and that He will answer in keeping with His good purposes.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 11:22-24; 1 John 5:14-15; Matthew 7:11; Hebrews 11:6.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-20">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 20.</span> Does believing prayer make things happen by its own power?</h3></summary>
<p>A. No. Prayer has no power in itself; it is God alone who answers. Yet God has chosen to work through the prayers of His people, so prayer really does change things.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Psalm 115:3; Ezekiel 36:37; James 5:16-18.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-21">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 21.</span> What mistakes should we avoid in prayer?</h3></summary>
<p>A. There are two opposite mistakes. The first is presumptive optimism: assuming a result is guaranteed because we want it so much, or have claimed it so confidently. The second is presumptive pessimism: expecting the worst so as never to be disappointed, and calling it humility. Both fix their eyes on the outcome rather than on God.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: James 4:13-16; Proverbs 27:1; Psalm 77:7-10; Matthew 25:24-25.</em></p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="hope-in-god">
<summary><h2>Hope in God</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="q-22">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 22.</span> What is the Bible’s alternative to presumptive optimism and presumptive pessimism?</h3></summary>
<p>A. The Bible’s alternative is confidence in God: hope that rests on God Himself and His promises, rather than on any particular outcome. The Bible calls this hope an anchor of the soul, both sure and steadfast.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Psalm 42:5; Jeremiah 17:7; Hebrews 6:18-19; Hebrews 10:35; Romans 15:13.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-23">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 23.</span> How is biblical hope different from wishing, or from feeling that something is already ours?</h3></summary>
<p>A. In everyday speech, hope often means a wish. In the Bible, hope is a confident expectation resting on God’s character and promises. It is honest that we do not yet see what we hope for, and it waits patiently for God, rather than trying to bring things about by feeling.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Romans 8:24-25; Lamentations 3:22-26; Psalm 27:13.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-24">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 24.</span> What is biblical hope certain of, and what does it leave in God’s hands?</h3></summary>
<p>A. It is certain of God’s goodness and faithfulness, and that He will work all things together for good to those who love Him, making them like Christ and bringing them safely home to glory. But it leaves the outcome of each request in God’s hands, like the three young men in Babylon who said, in effect, “Our God is able to deliver us; but if not, we will still trust Him.” Pain is still pain, and the Bible never pretends otherwise; yet this hope can look pain in the face.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Romans 8:28-29; Daniel 3:17-18; Habakkuk 3:17-18; Job 13:15; Hebrews 12:11.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-25">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 25.</span> Does the size of our faith matter?</h3></summary>
<p>A. It isn’t the size of our faith that moves mountains, but the greatness of the God it rests on. Even faith as small as a grain of mustard seed is enough, when it rests on Him.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Matthew 17:20; Mark 11:22.</em></p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="how-god-answers">
<summary><h2>How God answers</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="q-26">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 26.</span> How does God answer the prayers of those who trust Him?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Sometimes God gives the very thing that was asked. Where He does not, He always answers the deepest heart of a prayer offered in faith, giving something better, which brings Him greater honour and does His people greater good.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: James 1:6-7; James 5:15-18; Mark 14:36; 2 Corinthians 12:8-9; Romans 8:28.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-27">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 27.</span> Are there things that believers may be sure of receiving exactly as they ask?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Yes. For the things God has expressly promised to all who trust Him, such as the forgiveness of their sins, His Holy Spirit, wisdom, and grace to follow Christ to the end, they may be sure of receiving the very thing they ask, for God cannot break His word. Even so, faith does not pretend to have what it does not yet have. If a trustworthy friend promises to post you a parcel, you may be as sure of receiving it as if it were already on your doorstep; yet it would be self-deception to believe it is already in your hands. In the same way, believing prayer rests its certainty on God’s faithfulness, not on feeling that the answer has already come. Other things, such as health, wealth or a particular outcome, God has not promised in particular; these His people ask with submission to His will, trusting Him to give either the thing itself or something better. In every case, whether the thing is promised or not, God chooses the time and the manner of His answer. Sometimes He answers at once, as when He pardons sins truly confessed; sometimes He gives little by little, or only after long waiting, to exercise His people’s faith and patience, to draw them closer to Himself, and to make His gifts the sweeter when they come.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: 1 John 1:9; Luke 11:13; James 1:5-6; Philippians 1:6; Numbers 23:19; Hebrews 6:17-18; 1 John 5:14-15; Matthew 6:33; Romans 8:24-25; James 1:3-4; Luke 18:7; Habakkuk 2:3.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-28">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 28.</span> Why does God sometimes refuse what we ask?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Sometimes it is because we have asked wrongly, misled by our own desires, by the world around us, or by the devil. Indeed, refusal can be a mercy: when people insist on what is not good for them, God sometimes gives it, but to their sorrow, as when Israel demanded flesh in the wilderness, and “he gave them their request; but sent leanness into their soul.” But sometimes we have asked rightly, and God simply has a greater purpose than we can yet see.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: James 4:3; Psalm 106:15; Numbers 11:18-20, 33; Mark 8:33; 2 Corinthians 12:7-9; Deuteronomy 29:29.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-29">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 29.</span> Does a Christian’s closeness to God make a difference?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Yes, as a general rule. The more closely a Christian walks with God, the more their desires are shaped by His Word, and so the more often their prayers agree with His will. But it is a general rule, not a promise for every case. Nor does closeness to God mean that a Christian will always understand what God is doing in their life. God Himself said of Job that there was none like him in the earth, yet Job was never told why he suffered. When God answered him, He gave him no reason, but showed him His own wisdom and power, and Job humbled himself before Him. So a “no”, or a long silence, is not in itself a sign that a Christian has asked wrongly, or that they are far from God.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: John 15:7; 1 John 3:22; James 5:16; Job 1:8; Job 30:20; Job 38:1-4; Job 42:1-6; Mark 14:36.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-30">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 30.</span> Does God ever leave the prayers of His people unanswered?</h3></summary>
<p>A. No prayer offered in faith goes unheard. Sometimes God gives what was asked at once; sometimes He refuses the thing asked; and sometimes He says wait, perhaps for many years. Yet even while His people wait, their prayers have been heard from the first day. The saints in Scripture sometimes felt that God was silent, and they told Him so; such honest crying to Him is itself an act of faith, and He bears long with His people until the time He has appointed.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: 1 John 5:14-15; Luke 1:13; Daniel 10:12-13; Luke 18:1-8; Psalm 13:1-6; Psalm 22:1-2; Habakkuk 2:3.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-31">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 31.</span> What does Jesus’ prayer in Gethsemane teach us?</h3></summary>
<p>A. It teaches that real faith asks fully, trusts fully and submits fully. Jesus prayed perfectly, yet His request that the cup of suffering be taken away was refused. The deepest heart of His prayer, “not what I will, but what thou wilt”, was granted in full. Earlier that same night He had prayed, “Father, the hour is come; glorify thy Son, that thy Son also may glorify thee”, and through the cross He gained the joy set before Him: first the glory of His Father, and with it the salvation of His people. In that prayer He had also asked the Father to keep those whom He had given Him, and in the garden itself that request was answered at once. When He was arrested, He told the soldiers to let His disciples go, “That the saying might be fulfilled, which he spake, Of them which thou gavest me have I lost none.” So in the same garden one request was refused and another was granted, and both served the same great purpose.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 14:36; Luke 22:42-44; John 17:1-5; John 17:11-12; John 17:24; John 18:1; John 18:8-9; Hebrews 5:7; Hebrews 12:2.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-32">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 32.</span> Did Jesus “have” what He asked for in Gethsemane?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Yes, in the deepest sense. Set beside Mark 11:24, His prayer reads like this: “What things soever ye desire <span class="gloss">[take away this cup from me]</span>, when ye pray, believe that ye receive <em>them</em> <span class="gloss">[Abba, Father, all things are possible unto thee]</span>, and ye shall have <em>them</em> <span class="gloss">[nevertheless not what I will, but what thou wilt]</span>.” Scripture tells us that He prayed “unto him that was able to save him from death, and was heard in that he feared”. He was not spared from death, but He was saved out of it, rising again the third day. That was exactly what His Father willed, and so it was what Jesus Himself most truly desired. We hear that desire in His own words soon afterwards, as He was arrested: “the cup which my Father hath given me, shall I not drink it?”</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 14:36; John 18:11; Hebrews 5:7; Acts 2:24.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-33">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 33.</span> Is this promise only for individuals?</h3></summary>
<p>A. No. Jesus spoke to His disciples together, so the promise belongs to His people as a whole: to Christian nations, churches and families, as well as to each believer.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 11:22-24; Matthew 28:19-20; Matthew 18:19-20; Acts 4:24-31; Acts 12:5.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-34">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 34.</span> What comfort does God give to those who are still waiting?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Waiting is not being forgotten. When Jesus heard that His friend Lazarus was sick, He stayed where He was two days longer, yet Scripture tells us first that He loved him and his sisters. His delay was not a lack of love, but love with a greater purpose. And when He came, He wept with them, even though He was about to raise Lazarus from the dead. God has said that a mother may forget her child, yet He will not forget His people. So keep praying, and do not lose heart; those who wait on the LORD will find their strength renewed. The time may be long, but no moment of it is wasted, and He has not stopped caring for you while you wait.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: John 11:5-6, 33-35, 40; Isaiah 49:15-16; Luke 18:1; Isaiah 40:31; Psalm 27:14; Galatians 6:9.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-35">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 35.</span> What comfort does God give when He refuses what His people ask?</h3></summary>
<p>A. They have a Saviour who knows what it is to have a request refused. In Gethsemane He asked that the cup be taken from Him, and it was not; yet the deepest heart of His prayer, “not what I will, but what thou wilt”, was granted in full. Only hours before, He had prayed, “And now, O Father, glorify thou me with thine own self with the glory which I had with thee before the world was.” The cup He was not spared was the very way to that glory, and the Father granted it, raising Him from the dead and exalting Him to His right hand. So for His people too, a refused request can be the road to something far better. He is touched with the feeling of our weaknesses, and He does not ask His people to pretend that a refusal does not hurt. God is near to the broken-hearted, and He keeps account of every tear. When Paul begged three times for his thorn to be taken away, God refused, but gave him something better: “My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.” The thing that was asked for may never be given in this life, but God gives Himself, and He is the strength of His people’s hearts and their portion for ever. One day He will wipe away all tears from their eyes.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 14:36; Luke 22:42; John 17:5; John 18:1, 11; Philippians 2:8-9; Hebrews 12:2; Hebrews 4:15; Psalm 34:18; Psalm 56:8; 2 Corinthians 12:7-10; Psalm 73:25-26; Revelation 21:4.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-36">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 36.</span> When will Christians fully understand God’s answers to their prayers?</h3></summary>
<p>A. In glory. There, with hearts and wills made perfect, they will see that what God gave them was what they most truly wanted all along. As Jesus said, “What I do thou knowest not now; but thou shalt know hereafter.”</p>
<p><em>Bible references: John 13:7; 1 Corinthians 13:12; Psalm 17:15.</em></p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="why-gods-will-is-best">
<summary><h2>Why God’s will is best</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="q-37">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 37.</span> Why is it better that God’s will be done than our own?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Because God knows everything, and we know very little. We cannot see the future, or even the whole of the present, and we cannot always tell what would truly be good for us or for those we love. Nor are our hearts as trustworthy as we like to think: the Bible says they are deceitful above all things, and many things we long for would harm us if we had them. And even if we knew what was best, our power to bring it about is small, and it is not our own. We can do many things, but only as God gives us life and breath and strength. We cannot “make one hair white or black”, or be sure of tomorrow; and to save ourselves from our sin lies infinitely beyond us. God, by contrast, is infinite in both power and wisdom: “Great <em>is</em> our Lord, and of great power: his understanding <em>is</em> infinite.” He is perfectly good and full of love; He sees the end from the beginning, and He works all things together for good to those who love Him. To insist on our own will rather than His is like sailors in a storm rising up against a captain who knows every rock and current, and who alone can bring them to their “desired haven”.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Psalm 147:5; Isaiah 55:8-9; Jeremiah 17:9; Proverbs 14:12; Acts 17:25; Matthew 5:36; James 4:13-15; Luke 12:16-20, 25-26; Romans 5:6; Jeremiah 10:12; Daniel 2:20; Isaiah 46:9-10; Romans 8:28; Romans 11:33; Psalm 107:29-30.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-38">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 38.</span> Is it something new to want our own will rather than God’s?</h3></summary>
<p>A. No. It is the oldest temptation of all. In the garden of Eden the serpent promised the first man and woman, “ye shall be as gods”, if they would take what God had forbidden. They chose their own will over His, and sin, sorrow and death came into the world. Every attempt to make ourselves the centre and source of our own good follows the same path. True freedom is not freedom from God, but being what He made us to be. A fish that flings itself onto the bank to be free of the water finds not freedom, but death. In the same way, we were made, as the Westminster Shorter Catechism puts it, “to glorify God, and to enjoy him for ever”; and only in trusting and obeying Him do we find true freedom and joy.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Genesis 3:1-6; Romans 5:12; Jeremiah 10:23; Proverbs 3:5-7; Acts 17:28; 1 Corinthians 10:31; Psalm 16:11; Psalm 119:45; John 8:36.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-39">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 39.</span> Where can we see most clearly that God’s will is good?</h3></summary>
<p>A. At the cross. There God willed that His Son should suffer and die for sinners. To human eyes it looked like the worst thing that could ever happen; yet through it God gave His unspeakable gift: His own Son, who bore the full weight of divine wrath, so that all who trust in Him, though they were God’s enemies, might be rescued from their sins, and from the eternal wrath due to them. Nor is that all, for Christ is “the Saviour of the world”. Through the cross, God will one day set His whole creation free from the confusion, misery, pain and death that came into the world through Adam’s sin, and He will make all things new. And if God did not spare His own Son, we can trust Him with everything else: “He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?” The greatest of those “all things” is God Himself. In Christ He gives Himself to His people, to be their God for ever. In the new heavens and the new earth they will see His face and dwell with Him, together with His holy angels and all His redeemed people from every nation, in a joy that will never end.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Isaiah 53:5-6, 10; Matthew 1:21; Acts 2:23; Acts 4:27-28; 2 Corinthians 9:15; Romans 5:8-10; John 4:42; 1 John 4:14; Romans 8:19-23; Colossians 1:19-20; Revelation 21:4-5; Romans 8:32; Revelation 21:3; 2 Peter 3:13; Revelation 22:3-5; Hebrews 12:22-23; Revelation 7:9; Psalm 16:11.</em></p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="mark-1124-in-sum">
<summary><h2>Mark 11:24 in sum</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="q-40">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 40.</span> So what, in sum, does Mark 11:24 mean?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Jesus said, “Therefore I say unto you, What things soever ye desire, when ye pray, believe that ye receive <em>them</em>, and ye shall have <em>them</em>.” Taken a phrase at a time, this is what He meant:</p>
<ul>
<li>“I say unto you”: He is speaking to those who trust God.</li>
<li>“What things soever ye desire”: whatever they long for, once God Himself has shaped their wishes so that His will comes first.</li>
<li>“believe that ye receive <em>them</em>”: they rest on God to answer, not on how strongly they feel.</li>
<li>“ye shall have <em>them</em>”: God will grant what He is teaching them most truly to desire: that His will be done, rather than their own. Sometimes that means giving the very thing asked for, when it serves His wise and loving purposes. Sometimes, as with Jesus in Gethsemane, it means refusing the very thing asked for, yet granting the deepest heart of the prayer, giving something far better; and one day they will see that it was what they most truly wanted.</li>
</ul>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 11:22-24; Philippians 2:13; Romans 8:26-27; Mark 14:36; John 13:7.</em></p>
</details>
</details>
<details class="part" id="the-good-news">
<summary><h2>The good news</h2></summary>
<details class="head" id="q-41">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 41.</span> Who is Jesus?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Jesus is the eternal Son of God, who became a real man without ceasing to be God. He lived a perfect life, never once sinning, and He is the only Saviour of the world.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: John 1:1, 14; Colossians 2:9; Hebrews 4:15; Acts 4:12.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-42">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 42.</span> What is our great need?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Every one of us has sinned against God, falling short of His glory and breaking His law in thought, word and deed. That law is summed up in the Ten Commandments. These sins spring from a sinful nature we have had from the very start of our lives, passed down from Adam, the first man. Our sin separates us from God, and the just penalty it deserves is death, and the eternal punishment of hell. We cannot put this right ourselves, nor change our own hearts.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Romans 3:10, 23; Exodus 20:1-17; Psalm 51:5; Romans 5:12; Jeremiah 17:9; Isaiah 59:2; Romans 6:23; Galatians 3:10; Mark 9:43-49; Revelation 20:11-15.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-43">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 43.</span> What has Jesus done about it?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Jesus died on the cross in the place of sinners, bearing the curse and punishment their sins deserved. Then God raised Him from the dead the third day, showing that the debt was paid in full.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Galatians 3:13; 1 Peter 3:18; 1 Corinthians 15:3-4; Romans 4:25.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-44">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 44.</span> How can I receive this salvation?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Salvation is God’s free gift, which cannot be earned. You receive it by turning from your sin and trusting in Jesus Christ alone. Whoever believes in Him will not perish, but have everlasting life.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: Mark 1:15; Acts 16:31; Ephesians 2:8-9; John 3:16; Romans 10:9-13.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-45">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 45.</span> Will Jesus receive me, whatever I have done?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Yes. Jesus promises, “Him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.” He invites all who are weary and burdened to come to Him for rest, and no sin is too great for His blood to cleanse.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: John 6:37; Matthew 11:28-30; Isaiah 1:18; 1 John 1:7-9; 1 Timothy 1:15.</em></p>
</details>
<details class="head" id="q-46">
<summary><h3><span class="para-no q-no">Q. 46.</span> What should I do now?</h3></summary>
<p>A. Don’t put it off, for “behold, now <em>is</em> the accepted time; behold, now <em>is</em> the day of salvation.” Come to Christ today, and ask Him for mercy as the tax collector did: “God be merciful to me a sinner.” Read the Gospels to get to know Him, and search the Scriptures daily, asking His blessing on your reading and testing everything you hear by them, for not everyone who speaks in Christ’s name speaks His truth.</p>
<p>So be careful whom you listen to. A church is not faithful just because it is friendly, lively or popular, or even because it preaches about Jesus. Ask whether it worships God only in the way He has commanded in His Word, without adding inventions of its own.</p>
<p>Ask whether it holds to the whole of His truth as summarised in the Westminster Confession of Faith and Catechisms; whether it teaches that nations and churches are bound to enter into covenant with the Lord; and whether, where such covenants have already been made, such as the National Covenant and the Solemn League and Covenant, it acknowledges that they still bind those nations and churches today.</p>
<p>The National Covenant was sworn by Scotland, and the Solemn League and Covenant by Scotland, England and Ireland. These covenants bind those nations. They also bind the lands that were once their colonies and have since become nations in their own right, such as the United States, Canada, Australia and New Zealand. And they bind the churches of those nations, and the churches descended from them.</p>
<p>Ancient Israel was the first nation to covenant with God in this way (Exodus 24:7), and its covenant, sworn at Sinai and renewed in later generations, still binds its people, though they have broken it (Deuteronomy 29:14-15). Most other nations have not yet covenanted with Him, but the day is coming when they will, for the prophets foretold that the nations would join themselves to the LORD and vow their vows to Him (Isaiah 19:21; Zechariah 2:11).</p>
<p>Ask whether it submits to Christ alone as King and Head of His Church, governing its worship, doctrine and discipline by His Word, or whether it allows kings, parliaments or governments to rule over these things (an error known as Erastianism), or shapes its teaching to suit the opinions of the world.</p>
<p>Many churches today have fallen far from these things, and to sit under their ministry, even without meaning to, is to lend your support to what they have done. This is not to say that they are no churches at all, or that there are no true Christians in them. God has many of His people in them, beloved in the LORD, and we love them as brothers and sisters in Christ. But faithfulness to Christ means we cannot join in what He has not approved, however dear those who practise it may be. So don’t settle for less than Christ requires.</p>
<p>Meanwhile, worship God privately and in your home. Pray, read His Word, sing His praise from the Psalms, and keep His Sabbath holy. You can also learn from faithful ministers of the past through their writings and recorded sermons. Ask God to lead you to others who fear Him and want to walk in the old paths, so that you may encourage one another. And pray earnestly that He would restore His Church to purity.</p>
<p>Count the cost, for “if any <em>man</em> will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.” Then you too may pray with faith in God, and find in Him a hope that will never disappoint you.</p>
<p><em>Bible references: 2 Corinthians 6:2; Luke 18:13; John 20:31; Psalm 119:18; Acts 17:11; 1 John 4:1; Deuteronomy 12:32; 2 Samuel 21:1-2; Isaiah 19:18-21; Micah 4:1-2; Zechariah 2:11; Colossians 1:18; Acts 5:29; Revelation 3:4; 1 John 5:1; Jeremiah 6:16; Malachi 3:16; Matthew 16:24; Romans 5:5.</em></p>
</details>
</details>
