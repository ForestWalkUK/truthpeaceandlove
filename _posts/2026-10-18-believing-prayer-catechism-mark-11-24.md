> **“Therefore I say unto you, What things soever ye desire, when ye pray, believe that ye receive *them*, and ye shall have *them*.”**
>
> — Mark 11:24

Few verses in the Bible are quoted as often, or in as many different ways, as this one. Law of Attraction and manifesting teachers often cite it as though Jesus were teaching that focused belief, on its own, has the power to shape outcomes: if you can feel that something is already yours, it must come to you. The Word of Faith movement often reads it in a similar way, as though a believer’s confident faith and spoken words could secure health, prosperity or whatever is asked.

Taken on its own, out of context, I can see why the verse might be read like that. But I don’t believe that is what Jesus meant, and I believe what He actually meant is far better news.

This catechism is my attempt to explain the verse biblically, letting the events around it in Mark chapter 11, and the rest of Scripture, show what Jesus was saying. It follows the question-and-answer pattern of the Westminster catechisms, but in plain modern English, with Bible references after each answer so that you can check everything for yourself. Whether you are a Christian, someone exploring manifesting, or simply curious, you are very welcome here. If you are reading on a desktop computer, you can hover over any Bible reference to read the verse itself.

## The setting: Jerusalem, and God’s patience

**Q. 1. What does Jesus promise in Mark 11:24?**

A. Jesus promises His followers that whatever they ask for in prayer, trusting that they receive it, they will have.

*Bible references: Mark 11:24.*

**Q. 2. When and where did Jesus make this promise?**

A. Jesus made this promise in Jerusalem, in the last week before His death. He had just entered the city as its promised King, cursed a fig tree that was full of leaves but bore no fruit, and driven the traders out of the temple.

*Bible references: Zechariah 9:9; Mark 11:1-21.*

**Q. 3. Who was it that came to Jerusalem that week?**

A. It was the Lord Jesus Christ, the eternal Son of God, who became man. He came not to judge the world but to save it: to be crucified for sinners, and to rise again from the dead the third day. Yet He came also as the Lord of His temple, and He wept over the city as He warned it of the judgement that would fall on it for rejecting Him.

*Bible references: Malachi 3:1; John 1:14; Galatians 4:4; 1 Timothy 3:16; John 3:17; John 12:47-48; Mark 10:33-34; Luke 19:41-44.*

**Q. 4. Why had God formed the nation of Israel?**

A. God called Abraham and promised that through his family all the families of the earth would be blessed. Israel was the nation through which God would give His Word to the world, and from which the Saviour Himself would be born.

*Bible references: Genesis 12:1-3; Romans 3:1-2; Romans 9:4-5.*

**Q. 5. How did God show His patience towards Israel?**

A. Over many centuries, God rescued Israel from slavery in Egypt, gave them a good land, and kept sending His prophets to call them back to Himself, early and late, because He had compassion on them. For many years He bore with them.

*Bible references: Exodus 20:2; Jeremiah 7:25; 2 Chronicles 36:15; Nehemiah 9:30; Isaiah 5:1-4.*

**Q. 6. How did Israel respond to God’s patience?**

A. Again and again they turned away. Within weeks of leaving Egypt they worshipped a golden calf. Later they served other gods, even sacrificing their own children to them. They mocked God’s messengers, despised His words, and killed many of His prophets.

*Bible references: Exodus 32:1-8; Jeremiah 7:31; Psalm 106:37-38; 2 Chronicles 36:16; Nehemiah 9:26; Acts 7:51-52.*

**Q. 7. How did they respond when God’s own Son came to them?**

A. They rejected Him too. He came to His own, and His own did not receive Him. His home town tried to throw Him from a cliff; the religious leaders said His miracles were the work of the devil, and for a long time they had been plotting to kill Him. On the very day He cleansed the temple, they looked for a way to destroy Him.

*Bible references: John 1:11; Luke 4:28-30; Mark 3:6, 22; John 11:53; Mark 11:18.*

**Q. 8. What did they do in the days that followed?**

A. Within days they had Him arrested, condemned and crucified, just as He had foretold in His parable of the vineyard owner’s beloved son. Yet even on the cross He prayed for them, “Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do,” and soon afterwards thousands of them repented and were saved.

*Bible references: Mark 12:1-9; Mark 14:1, 43-64; Mark 15:12-15; Luke 23:34; Acts 2:23, 36-41.*

**Q. 9. What does the withered fig tree picture?**

A. It pictures God’s judgement on a people who looked religious outwardly but bore no fruit for God, after all His patience. That judgement came in two stages: God gave the nation over to the blindness it had chosen, and later, in A.D. 70, Jerusalem and its temple were destroyed. Yet God did not cast away every one of them.

*Bible references: Jeremiah 8:13; John 12:37-40; Luke 19:43-44; Romans 11:1-2.*

**Q. 10. What does this history mean for us?**

A. It is a mirror for every one of us. God shows the same patience to us, and His kindness is meant to lead us to turn back to Him. But His patience is not endless, and it is not to be presumed upon. While He waits, His call stands open: “To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts.”

*Bible references: Romans 2:4-5; Romans 11:20-22; Hebrews 3:15; 2 Peter 3:15.*

## Faith in God

**Q. 11. What does the withered fig tree teach us about this promise?**

A. The fig tree withered at Jesus’ word alone. It shows that the power behind this promise is God’s power, not ours, and that our trust is to be in God Himself, not in how strongly we believe.

*Bible references: Mark 11:20-23; Psalm 33:9; Luke 1:37.*

**Q. 12. What is the first thing Jesus asks of those who pray?**

A. Jesus begins with the words “Have faith in God.” Faith in God, rather than in ourselves or in the universe, is where all true prayer starts, for without faith it is impossible to please Him.

*Bible references: Mark 11:22; Hebrews 11:6; Romans 1:25.*

**Q. 13. Is this promise made to everyone?**

A. No. Jesus made this promise to His own disciples, who trusted in Him. The Bible is honest that God does not welcome the prayers of those who turn their backs on Him and His Word. That is a sobering truth, and by nature it is true of every one of us.

*Bible references: Mark 11:22; John 14:13-14; Proverbs 15:8; Proverbs 28:9.*

**Q. 14. Is there any prayer God will hear from someone who does not yet know Him?**

A. Yes, and gladly. God has promised to hear anyone, from anywhere, who turns to Him and asks for mercy, like the tax collector who prayed, “God be merciful to me a sinner.” No one who comes to Him in that way is ever turned away.

*Bible references: Luke 18:13-14; Romans 10:13; Isaiah 55:6-7; John 6:37.*

**Q. 15. Why does God accept the prayers of those who trust Him?**

A. God accepts them only because of what Jesus has done. Jesus took upon Himself the curse that our wrongdoing deserved, so that all who trust Him can come to God as their Father.

*Bible references: Galatians 3:13; John 14:13-14; Ephesians 2:18; Hebrews 4:14-16.*

**Q. 16. What should we ask for in prayer?**

A. The whole Bible guides us in prayer, but the special rule is the prayer Jesus taught His disciples, called the Lord’s Prayer. It may be prayed as it stands, with understanding and faith, and it is also the pattern for all our other prayers. It teaches us to ask first for the things that matter most to God: that His name be hallowed, that is, honoured as holy; that His kingdom come; and that His will be done on earth, as it is in heaven. Then it teaches us to ask for what we need: our daily bread, the forgiveness of our sins as we forgive others, and to be kept from temptation and delivered from evil. Following that pattern, we may ask for anything that serves God’s glory and the good of His people, for others as well as for ourselves, but never for anything His Word forbids.

*Bible references: Matthew 6:9-13, 33; Luke 11:1-4; 1 Timothy 2:1-2; Philippians 2:4; 1 John 5:14.*

**Q. 17. Where do right desires in prayer come from?**

A. They come from God Himself. He works in His people, shaping both what they want and what they do, and His Spirit helps them to pray when they don’t know what to ask.

*Bible references: Philippians 2:13; Psalm 10:17; Romans 8:26-27.*

**Q. 18. Are all the desires of Christians from God?**

A. No. Christians still struggle with wrong desires, and their prayers are often a mixture of what God has put in their hearts and what they simply want for themselves. Even Peter, who loved Jesus dearly, was once rebuked for seeing things from a merely human point of view.

*Bible references: Mark 8:33; Galatians 5:17; James 4:3.*

**Q. 19. What does it mean to “believe that ye receive”?**

A. It means trusting God Himself, His power, goodness, wisdom and faithfulness, rather than the strength of our feelings or the outcome we hope for. It is being confident that He hears us, and that He will answer in keeping with His good purposes.

*Bible references: Mark 11:22-24; 1 John 5:14-15; Matthew 7:11; Hebrews 11:6.*

**Q. 20. Does believing prayer make things happen by its own power?**

A. No. Prayer has no power in itself; it is God alone who answers. Yet God has chosen to work through the prayers of His people, so prayer really does change things.

*Bible references: Psalm 115:3; Ezekiel 36:37; James 5:16-18.*

**Q. 21. What mistakes should we avoid in prayer?**

A. There are two opposite mistakes. The first is presumptive optimism: assuming a result is guaranteed because we want it so much, or have claimed it so confidently. The second is presumptive pessimism: expecting the worst so as never to be disappointed, and calling it humility. Both fix their eyes on the outcome rather than on God.

*Bible references: James 4:13-16; Proverbs 27:1; Psalm 77:7-10; Matthew 25:24-25.*

## Hope in God

**Q. 22. What is the Bible’s alternative to presumptive optimism and presumptive pessimism?**

A. The Bible’s alternative is confidence in God: hope that rests on God Himself and His promises, rather than on any particular outcome. The Bible calls this hope an anchor of the soul, both sure and steadfast.

*Bible references: Psalm 42:5; Jeremiah 17:7; Hebrews 6:18-19; Hebrews 10:35; Romans 15:13.*

**Q. 23. How is biblical hope different from wishing, or from feeling that something is already ours?**

A. In everyday speech, hope often means a wish. In the Bible, hope is a confident expectation resting on God’s character and promises. It is honest that we do not yet see what we hope for, and it waits patiently for God, rather than trying to bring things about by feeling.

*Bible references: Romans 8:24-25; Lamentations 3:22-26; Psalm 27:13.*

**Q. 24. What is biblical hope certain of, and what does it leave in God’s hands?**

A. It is certain of God’s goodness and faithfulness, and that He will work all things together for good to those who love Him, making them like Christ and bringing them safely home to glory. But it leaves the details of each request in God’s hands, like the three young men in Babylon who said, in effect, “Our God is able to deliver us; but if not, we will still trust Him.” Pain is still pain, and the Bible never pretends otherwise; yet this hope can look pain in the face.

*Bible references: Romans 8:28-29; Daniel 3:17-18; Habakkuk 3:17-18; Job 13:15; Hebrews 12:11.*

**Q. 25. Does the size of our faith matter?**

A. It isn’t the size of our faith that moves mountains, but the greatness of the God it rests on. Even faith as small as a grain of mustard seed is enough, when it rests on Him.

*Bible references: Matthew 17:20; Mark 11:22.*

## How God answers

**Q. 26. How does God answer the prayers of those who trust Him?**

A. Sometimes God answers in the very details of what was asked. Where He does not, He always answers the deepest heart of the prayer, giving something better, which brings Him greater honour and does His people greater good.

*Bible references: James 5:17-18; Mark 14:36; 2 Corinthians 12:8-9; Romans 8:28.*

**Q. 27. Why does God sometimes say no to the details?**

A. Sometimes it is because we have asked wrongly, misled by our own desires, by the world around us, or by the devil. But sometimes we have asked rightly, and God simply has a greater purpose than we can yet see.

*Bible references: James 4:3; Mark 8:33; 2 Corinthians 12:7-9; Deuteronomy 29:29.*

**Q. 28. Does a Christian’s closeness to God make a difference?**

A. Yes, as a general rule. The more closely a Christian walks with God, the more their desires are shaped by His Word, and so the more often their prayers agree with His will. But it is a general rule, not a promise for every case. Nor does closeness to God mean that a Christian will always understand what God is doing in their life. God Himself said of Job that there was none like him in the earth, yet Job was never told why he suffered. When God answered him, He gave him no reason, but showed him His own wisdom and power, and Job humbled himself before Him. So a “no”, or a long silence, is not in itself a sign that a Christian has asked wrongly, or that they are far from God.

*Bible references: John 15:7; 1 John 3:22; James 5:16; Job 1:8; Job 30:20; Job 38:1-4; Job 42:1-6; Mark 14:36.*

**Q. 29. What does Jesus’ prayer in Gethsemane teach us?**

A. It teaches that real faith asks fully, trusts fully and submits fully. Jesus prayed perfectly, yet His request that the cup of suffering be taken away was refused. The deepest heart of His prayer, “not what I will, but what thou wilt”, was granted in full. Earlier that same night He had prayed, “Father, the hour is come; glorify thy Son, that thy Son also may glorify thee”, and through the cross He gained the joy set before Him: first the glory of His Father, and with it the salvation of His people.

*Bible references: Mark 14:36; Luke 22:42-44; John 17:1-5; John 17:24; John 18:1; Hebrews 5:7; Hebrews 12:2.*

**Q. 30. Did Jesus “have” what He asked for in Gethsemane?**

A. Yes, in the deepest sense. Set beside Mark 11:24, His prayer reads like this: “What things soever ye desire <span class="gloss">[take away this cup from me]</span>, when ye pray, believe that ye receive *them* <span class="gloss">[Abba, Father, all things are possible unto thee]</span>, and ye shall have *them* <span class="gloss">[nevertheless not what I will, but what thou wilt]</span>.” Scripture tells us that He prayed “unto him that was able to save him from death, and was heard in that he feared”. He was not spared from death, but He was saved out of it, rising again the third day. That was exactly what His Father willed, and so it was what Jesus Himself most truly desired.

*Bible references: Mark 14:36; Hebrews 5:7; Acts 2:24.*

**Q. 31. Is this promise only for individuals?**

A. No. Jesus spoke to His disciples together, so the promise belongs to His people as a whole: to Christian nations, churches and families, as well as to each believer.

*Bible references: Mark 11:22-24; Matthew 28:19-20; Matthew 18:19-20; Acts 4:24-31; Acts 12:5.*

**Q. 32. When will Christians fully understand God’s answers to their prayers?**

A. In glory. There, with hearts and wills made perfect, they will see that what God gave them was what they most truly wanted all along. As Jesus said, “What I do thou knowest not now; but thou shalt know hereafter.”

*Bible references: John 13:7; 1 Corinthians 13:12; Psalm 17:15.*

**Q. 33. So what, in sum, does Mark 11:24 mean?**

A. With its meaning drawn out in square brackets, the verse reads like this: “Therefore I say unto you <span class="gloss">[who have faith in God]</span>, What things soever ye desire <span class="gloss">[savouring first and foremost the things of God: desires which God Himself works in you who trust Him]</span>, when ye pray, believe that ye receive *them* <span class="gloss">[resting not on the strength of your conviction, but on Him who prepares the heart to pray]</span>, and ye shall have *them* <span class="gloss">[for what God works in you to will, He also works to do, of His good pleasure. Sometimes this is in the very detail of your asking. Sometimes, as in Gethsemane, He answers the deepest substance of your prayer but not its specific details, refusing your present wish for something far better, more to God’s honour and the good of His people, which in glory you will see was what you most truly wanted]</span>.”

*Bible references: Mark 11:22-24; Philippians 2:13; Mark 14:36; John 13:7.*

## The good news

**Q. 34. Who is Jesus?**

A. Jesus is the eternal Son of God, who became a real man without ceasing to be God. He lived a perfect life, never once sinning, and He is the only Saviour of the world.

*Bible references: John 1:1, 14; Colossians 2:9; Hebrews 4:15; Acts 4:12.*

**Q. 35. What is our great need?**

A. Every one of us has sinned against God, falling short of His glory and breaking His law in thought, word and deed. Our sin separates us from God, and the just penalty it deserves is death, and the eternal punishment of hell. We cannot put this right ourselves.

*Bible references: Romans 3:10, 23; Exodus 20:1-17; Isaiah 59:2; Romans 6:23; Galatians 3:10.*

**Q. 36. What has Jesus done about it?**

A. Jesus died on the cross in the place of sinners, bearing the curse and punishment their sins deserved. Then God raised Him from the dead the third day, showing that the debt was paid in full.

*Bible references: Galatians 3:13; 1 Peter 3:18; 1 Corinthians 15:3-4; Romans 4:25.*

**Q. 37. How can I receive this salvation?**

A. Salvation is God’s free gift, which cannot be earned. You receive it by turning from your sin and trusting in Jesus Christ alone. Whoever believes in Him will not perish, but have everlasting life.

*Bible references: Mark 1:15; Acts 16:31; Ephesians 2:8-9; John 3:16; Romans 10:9-13.*

**Q. 38. Will Jesus receive me, whatever I have done?**

A. Yes. Jesus promises, “Him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.” He invites all who are weary and burdened to come to Him for rest, and no sin is too great for His blood to cleanse.

*Bible references: John 6:37; Matthew 11:28-30; Isaiah 1:18; 1 John 1:7-9; 1 Timothy 1:15.*

**Q. 39. What should I do now?**

A. Don’t put it off, for “behold, now *is* the accepted time; behold, now *is* the day of salvation.” Come to Christ today, and ask Him for mercy as the tax collector did: “God be merciful to me a sinner.” Read the Gospels to get to know Him, and search the Scriptures daily, asking His blessing on your reading and testing everything you hear by them, for not everyone who speaks in Christ’s name speaks His truth.

So be careful whom you listen to. A church is not faithful just because it is friendly, lively or popular, or even because it preaches about Jesus. Ask whether it worships God only in the way He has commanded in His Word, without adding inventions of its own. Ask whether it holds to the whole of His truth as summarised in the Westminster Confession of Faith and Catechisms, and whether it acknowledges that the covenants by which nations and churches have bound themselves to God, such as Scotland’s National Covenant and the Solemn League and Covenant, still bind them today. These covenants were sworn by Scotland, England and Ireland, and they bind those nations, the lands that were once their dominions and have since grown into nations of their own (such as the United States, Canada, Australia and New Zealand), and the churches descended from them. Other nations have not yet covenanted with God in this way, but the day is coming when they will, for the prophets foretold that the nations would join themselves to the LORD and vow their vows to Him. Ask whether it submits to Christ alone as King and Head of His Church, governing its worship, doctrine and discipline by His Word, or whether it allows kings, parliaments or governments to rule over these things (an error known as Erastianism), or shapes its teaching to suit the opinions of the world. Many churches today have fallen far from these things, and to sit under their ministry, even without meaning to, is to lend your support to what they have done. This is not to say that they are no churches at all, or that there are no true Christians in them. God has many of His people in them, beloved in the LORD, and we love them as brothers and sisters in Christ. But faithfulness to Christ means we cannot join in what He has not approved, however dear those who practise it may be. So don’t settle for less than Christ requires.

Meanwhile, worship God privately and in your home. Pray, read His Word, sing His praise from the Psalms, and keep His Sabbath holy. You can also learn from faithful ministers of the past through their writings and recorded sermons. Ask God to lead you to others who fear Him and want to walk in the old paths, so that you may encourage one another. And pray earnestly that He would restore His Church to purity.

Count the cost, for “if any *man* will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.” Then you too may pray with faith in God, and find in Him a hope that will never disappoint you.

*Bible references: 2 Corinthians 6:2; Luke 18:13; John 20:31; Psalm 119:18; Acts 17:11; 1 John 4:1; Deuteronomy 12:32; 2 Samuel 21:1-2; Isaiah 19:18-21; Micah 4:1-2; Zechariah 2:11; Colossians 1:18; Acts 5:29; Revelation 3:4; 1 John 5:1; Jeremiah 6:16; Malachi 3:16; Matthew 16:24; Romans 5:5.*
