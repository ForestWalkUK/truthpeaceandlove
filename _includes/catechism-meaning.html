Why questions and answers? (tap or click to read)

This study is written as questions and answers, a way of teaching so that the main truths of a subject can be learned, remembered and passed on. A book written in this way is called a catechism, and Christians have used catechisms for centuries, especially to teach those new to the faith, whether adults or children. Among the best known are the Westminster catechisms of the 1640s, whose pattern this study follows, in plain modern English. As a learner myself, I find catechisms extremely helpful in studying the Bible. The word itself comes from the Greek katēcheō, meaning to teach by word of mouth, literally to “sound down” into someone’s ears (it is related to our word “echo”). The New Testament uses this word of being taught the things of God: Luke wrote his account of Jesus’ life, his Gospel, so that Theophilus, the man he wrote it for, might know the certainty of the things “wherein thou hast been instructed” (Luke 1:4), where the Greek word behind “instructed” is katēcheō.
