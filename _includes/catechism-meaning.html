What does “catechism” mean? (tap or click to read)

A catechism is a way of teaching by questions and answers, so that the main truths of a subject can be learned, remembered and passed on. Christians have used catechisms in this way for centuries, especially to teach children and those new to the faith. The word itself comes from the Greek katēcheō, meaning to teach by word of mouth, literally to “sound down” into someone’s ears (it is related to our word “echo”). The New Testament uses it of being taught the things of God: Luke wrote his Gospel so that Theophilus might know the certainty of the things “wherein thou hast been instructed” (Luke 1:4).
